# The Longest Chapters in the Yerushalmi and Rambam: A Data-Driven Look

What are the longest chapters and sections in the Jerusalem Talmud and Maimonides' Mishneh Torah? We ran every chapter of both works through ChavrutAI's API and counted the Hebrew words. Here's what we found.

---

## The Jerusalem Talmud (Yerushalmi)

The Yerushalmi contains roughly **812,000 Hebrew words** across 297 chapters and 39 tractates. The average chapter runs about 2,735 words — but the range is enormous.

### Longest Chapter: Kiddushin, Chapter 1

At **12,834 words**, the first chapter of [Kiddushin](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Kiddushin/1) towers over everything else in the Yerushalmi. That's nearly double the next-longest chapter. Kiddushin 1 covers the foundational methods of betrothal and acquisition — topics that branch into long legal discussions touching property law, personal status, and the mechanics of kinyan (formal acquisition). The sheer breadth of the opening mishnah invites an unusually wide-ranging talmudic discussion.

### The Runners-Up

[Megillah Chapter 1](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Megillah/1) (10,445 words) and [Moed Katan Chapter 3](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Moed_Katan/3) (7,986 words) round out the top three. Several tractates place multiple chapters in the top 20: Berakhot contributes four chapters ([1](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Berakhot/1), [2](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Berakhot/2), [4](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Berakhot/4), and [9](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Berakhot/9)), Sanhedrin three ([1](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Sanhedrin/1), [7](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Sanhedrin/7), and [10](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Sanhedrin/10)), and Shabbat two ([1](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Shabbat/1) and [7](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Shabbat/7)). The longer chapters tend to cluster in tractates dealing with broad legal or aggadic themes — festivals, prayer, and criminal law.

### Longest Individual Section: Chagigah 2:10

When we zoom in from chapters to individual segments, the picture shifts. The longest single section in the Yerushalmi is **[Chagigah Chapter 2](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Chagigah/2), segment 10**, at **817 words**. Chagigah 2 is famous for its esoteric content — the "matters of the Chariot" (ma'aseh merkavah) — and this particular segment contains an extended mystical and aggadic passage. The next-longest segments come from [Taanit Chapter 4](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Taanit/4) (540 words, on the history of fast days) and another passage from [Chagigah 2](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Chagigah/2) (535 words).

Unlike chapter length, where a few tractates dominate, the longest individual segments are spread more evenly across the Yerushalmi. Sanhedrin, Rosh Hashanah, Pesachim, and Kiddushin all appear in the top 20, reflecting the Yerushalmi's tendency to insert long self-contained passages — often narrative or aggadic — within otherwise shorter halakhic discussions.

## Maimonides' Mishneh Torah (Rambam)

The Mishneh Torah is a very different kind of text. Maimonides wrote it as a systematic legal code: organized, precise, and deliberately concise. The entire work spans roughly **764,500 Hebrew words** across 1,001 chapters and 83 sections of Hilchot (legal topics), grouped into 14 books.

The average chapter is just **764 words** — less than a third of the Yerushalmi average. That brevity is by design; Maimonides prized clarity and economy of language.

### Longest Chapter: Tefillin, Mezuzah and the Torah Scroll, Chapter 8

The longest chapter in the entire Mishneh Torah is **[Hilchot Tefillin, Mezuzah and the Torah Scroll, Chapter 8](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Tefillin_Mezuzah_and_the_Torah_Scroll/8)**, at **3,104 words**. This chapter is dominated by a single extraordinary halakhah — [halakhah 4](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Tefillin_Mezuzah_and_the_Torah_Scroll/8#4), which alone contains **2,911 words**. That one passage details the precise forms of every Hebrew letter as they must appear in a Torah scroll, tefillin, and mezuzah: the exact shape of each letter, the required spacing, and which details invalidate the writing. It's essentially a calligrapher's manual embedded in a legal code.

At nearly 3,000 words, this single halakhah is longer than most entire chapters of the Rambam — and longer than many chapters of the Yerushalmi too.

### The Runners-Up

After that outlier, the next-longest chapters are more modest. [Sanctification of the New Month Chapter 17](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Sanctification_of_the_New_Month/17) (2,109 words) deals with the complex astronomical calculations for the Jewish calendar. [Divorce Chapter 9](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Divorce/9) (2,097 words) covers the detailed formulations and procedures for writing a get. These are chapters where Maimonides had to work through intricate procedural or technical material that resisted compression.

Sabbath law places three chapters in the top 20 ([17](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Sabbath/17), [21](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Sabbath/21), and [22](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Sabbath/22)), reflecting the sheer density of Shabbat regulations. Marriage and Forbidden Intercourse also appear repeatedly, as these areas of family law involve many detailed rulings.

### Longest Individual Halakhah: Tefillin 8:4

The longest individual halakhah is, unsurprisingly, the same passage that makes Chapter 8 the longest chapter: **[Tefillin 8:4](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Tefillin_Mezuzah_and_the_Torah_Scroll/8#4)** at **2,911 words**. The next-longest halakhah — [Sanhedrin 19:4](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/The_Sanhedrin_and_the_Penalties_within_Their_Jurisdiction/19#4) at 949 words — is less than a third its size. After that, values drop quickly: [Sanctification of the New Month 12:2](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Sanctification_of_the_New_Month/12#2) (601 words), [Service on the Day of Atonement 4:1](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Service_on_the_Day_of_Atonement/4#1) (521 words), and so on. The overwhelming majority of Rambam's halakhot are under 200 words. Tefillin 8:4 is a genuine anomaly.

---

## What the Numbers Tell Us

The contrast between these two works shows up clearly in the data. The Yerushalmi's longest chapter ([Kiddushin 1](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Kiddushin/1), 12,834 words) is four times the length of the Rambam's longest ([Tefillin 8](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Tefillin_Mezuzah_and_the_Torah_Scroll/8), 3,104 words). But at the section level the ratio narrows: the Yerushalmi's longest segment ([Chagigah 2:10](https://chavrutai.com/yerushalmi/Chagigah/2), 817 words) is still shorter than the Rambam's longest halakhah ([Tefillin 8:4](https://chavrutai.com/rambam/Tefillin_Mezuzah_and_the_Torah_Scroll/8#4), 2,911 words).

This makes sense. The Yerushalmi spreads its material across many short segments within long chapters; Maimonides concentrates legal detail into single dense halakhot within short chapters. One is a record of oral discourse, the other a composed legal reference work. The data reflects their fundamentally different literary characters.

# Technical Appendix: Methodology and Full Results

## Data Collection

Two TypeScript scripts queried every chapter of both works through ChavrutAI's REST API:

- **Yerushalmi:** `GET /api/yerushalmi/:tractate/:chapter` — 297 chapters across 39 tractates. Each API call proxies to Sefaria's API, fetching all halakhot within a chapter in parallel, then returning processed `hebrewSections[]`.
- **Rambam:** `GET /api/rambam/:hilchot/:chapter` — 1,001 chapters across 83 Hilchot sections. Each API call fetches a single bilingual chapter from Sefaria, returning processed `hebrewSections[]`.

## Word Counting

Hebrew word count was computed by:
1. Stripping all HTML tags from each section string
2. Replacing HTML entities with spaces
3. Matching all sequences of Hebrew Unicode characters (ranges U+0590–U+05FF, U+FB1D–U+FB4F)
4. Counting matches

This counts actual Hebrew words, excluding punctuation, numerals, and any embedded Latin-script text.
